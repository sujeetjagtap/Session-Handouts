# AI-Use Disclosure Templates
Snapshot date: 2026-10-04. Publisher policies change — verify against the journal's current author guide before submission (see `publisher_policy_cheatsheet.md` for how).

## The 4-part anatomy (works for every publisher)

A disclosure that survives editorial review states four things:

1. **WHICH TOOL** — name + version + month of use. *Example: ChatGPT (GPT-5), March 2026.*
2. **FOR WHAT** — the specific tasks and manuscript sections. *Example: improved phrasing in Sections 2 and 4; generated the first draft of the Figure 3 caption.*
3. **FOR WHAT NOT** — the negative space reviewers care most about. *Example: no role in study design, data collection, analysis, interpretation, or literature search.*
4. **HUMAN ROLE** — review and accountability. *Example: all output was reviewed, edited, and verified by the authors, who take full responsibility for the work.*

## Generic template (neutral wording, adapt per journal)

> **Declaration of generative AI use in scientific writing.** In preparing this manuscript, the authors used [tool name + version, month/year] to improve the readability and language of [sections/tasks]. The tool was not used to generate scientific content, perform data analysis or interpretation, conduct literature searches, or create study materials. All AI-assisted output was reviewed, edited, and verified by the authors, who take full responsibility for the integrity and content of this work.

## Elsevier variant (declaration placed **before the References**)

> **Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the writing process.** During the preparation of this work the author(s) used [tool + version] in order to [readability and language improvement of Sections 2 and 4; drafting of the Figure 3 caption]. After using this tool/service, the author(s) reviewed and edited the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility for the content of the published article.

## Springer Nature variant (documented in the **Methods** section, or the **Acknowledgements** if no Methods)

> **AI-assisted writing statement.** The authors used [tool + version] to assist with language editing and restructuring of this manuscript ([sections]). No content generated by the tool was used verbatim; all scientific content, data, analyses, and interpretations were produced and verified by the authors, who bear full responsibility for the work. The use of this tool is disclosed here in accordance with the journal's policy.

## IEEE variant (disclosed in the **Acknowledgments** section)

> **Acknowledgment of AI use.** [Tool + version] was used in the preparation of this manuscript for English language editing and for improving the clarity of Sections 3 and 4. The authors reviewed and edited all AI-assisted output and take full responsibility for the content of the paper. No part of the research itself — including data collection, analysis, or interpretation — involved the use of generative AI.

## ACM variant (per ACM policy: disclosure in creation of the work)

> **Disclosure of AI assistance.** The authors used [tool + version] as a writing aid for [tasks/sections] in the preparation of this work. The tool had no role in the research design, experiments, data analysis, or scientific conclusions. All AI-assisted text was reviewed, verified, and rewritten as needed by the authors, who assume full responsibility for the published work.

## Fill-in checklist (2 minutes, before submission)

- [ ] Tool name, version, and month recorded (check your chat history for the actual dates)
- [ ] Sections/tasks listed specifically — "improve the paper" is not a disclosure
- [ ] Negative space stated: what AI did NOT touch
- [ ] Human review + responsibility sentence present
- [ ] Placement matches THIS journal's current guide (pre-References / Methods / Acknowledgments)
- [ ] Co-authors have seen and approved the statement
